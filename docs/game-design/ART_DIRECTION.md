# Smart Parrot — Art Direction

## Visual Identity

Smart Parrot should feel warm, adventurous, handcrafted, and unmistakably its own.

The target is not to reproduce another game's assets or characters. The goal is to capture the emotional strengths of premium cozy 3D adventures:

- inviting stylisation
- expressive characters
- readable silhouettes
- rich lighting
- tactile materials
- lively environments
- joyful reward moments

## Art Keywords

- storybook travel
- cozy cinematic
- polished stylisation
- warm optimism
- playful realism
- magical everyday life
- global adventure

## Shape Language

### Characters

- rounded primary forms
- clear facial features
- slightly oversized hands, eyes, and accessories
- readable silhouettes at mobile size
- restrained detail around the face

### Environment

- architecture simplified into strong, recognisable shapes
- corners softened where appropriate
- props slightly exaggerated for readability
- signs and interactive objects visually distinct without looking like UI cards

### Pico

Pico should have:

- a compact pear-shaped body
- large expressive eyes
- broad wings that function like hands
- a distinctive dark beak silhouette
- green as the dominant body colour
- yellow and blue accent feathers
- a small travel accessory that changes by city

Pico must remain recognisable in silhouette without relying on a logo or text.

## Colour System

### Core brand colours

- Parrot green: lively, natural, optimistic
- Sun yellow: interaction, reward, warmth
- London navy: navigation, night scenes, premium contrast
- Sky blue: guidance, calm, transport
- Coral red: city energy and London accents

### Environment grading

Heathrow should use:

- cool architectural blues and greys
- warm sunrise or sunset light
- amber practical lights
- yellow interaction accents
- subtle green plant life to soften the terminal

Piccadilly Circus should use:

- deep evening blue
- warm shop windows
- saturated city signs
- wet-street reflections used sparingly

## Lighting

Lighting is a core gameplay tool.

- objectives receive gentle contrast, not giant glow effects
- faces remain readable in all dialogue scenes
- windows provide directional light and atmosphere
- practical lights should have visible sources
- reflective materials should remain controlled for mobile performance
- bloom should be subtle and reserved for reward moments, city lights, and key interactables

## Materials

Every major object should communicate material clearly:

- brushed metal gates
- glossy ceramic cups
- soft fabric luggage
- translucent glass walls
- rubber train flooring
- painted signage
- polished stone terminal floors

Avoid flat vector-style surfaces inside the 3D world.

## Character Animation Principles

- anticipation before important gestures
- small idle motion at all times
- eyes lead the head
- hands and wings reinforce dialogue
- reactions should be readable without audio
- celebration animation should feel earned, not constant

## Environment Animation

Heathrow needs ambient life:

- luggage belts moving
- departure board updates
- aircraft taxiing in the distance
- travellers walking with varied timing
- escalators and doors in motion
- subtle plant and fabric movement from ventilation

Piccadilly Circus needs:

- traffic loops
- crossing signals
- pedestrians
- changing illuminated displays
- light rain variation or post-rain reflections

## UI Direction

The HUD must protect the world.

Persistent UI:

- compact objective indicator
- small Pico status or help button
- minimal XP feedback

Transient UI:

- interaction prompt
- dialogue choices
- vocabulary discovery
- reward toast

Do not surround the playfield with large equal-weight panels.

### UI materials

- soft opaque surfaces rather than excessive glass blur
- rounded but not pill-shaped everywhere
- subtle bevel or inner highlight for a premium toy-like feel
- iconography that matches the 3D world's friendliness

## Camera Composition

- keep the player and Pico readable against the environment
- frame destinations using architecture and light
- avoid overhead mobile-game camera angles
- cinematic shots should be short and return control quickly

## Asset Quality Tiers

### Hero assets

Highest detail and animation budget:

- player character
- Pico
- Heathrow gate
- Underground roundel
- Piccadilly train
- key NPCs
- Piccadilly Circus arrival area

### Supporting assets

Moderate detail:

- luggage
- benches
- signs
- café props
- terminal plants
- barriers

### Background assets

Optimised silhouettes and textures:

- distant aircraft
- background travellers
- terminal architecture modules
- city traffic

## Originality Rules

- do not copy another game's characters, proportions, UI, logos, or exact environment layouts
- use references for mood, quality, lighting, and interaction clarity only
- build a recognisable Smart Parrot silhouette language
- use real-world London transport cues accurately where legally and practically appropriate

## Visual Review Checklist

Before approving a scene, confirm:

- Does it look like a game world rather than a webpage?
- Is the interactive path readable without giant labels?
- Are materials visibly different?
- Does Pico feel alive?
- Is the environment moving?
- Is the lighting emotionally intentional?
- Is the HUD occupying less visual weight than the world?
- Can the scene still be read on a phone screen?
