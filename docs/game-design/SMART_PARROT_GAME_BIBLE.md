# Smart Parrot — Game Design Bible

## Product Promise

**Do not learn English. Live it.**

Smart Parrot is a cozy 3D travel adventure in which the player explores the English-speaking world, builds relationships, completes quests, and becomes fluent by using English as a tool.

The player is never framed as a student. They are a traveller, newcomer, resident, and eventually a global citizen.

## Core Fantasy

The player arrives in an unfamiliar anglophone city with Pico, a lively parrot companion. Together they navigate transport, shops, friendships, work, culture, and everyday problems.

English is required to act in the world:

- understand signs and announcements
- ask for help
- buy food and tickets
- solve local problems
- form friendships
- unlock districts and cities

## Game Pillars

### 1. Live the language

Language learning happens through meaningful action rather than isolated exercises.

### 2. A world worth returning to

Each location must feel warm, alive, explorable, and emotionally memorable.

### 3. Pico is a companion, not a mascot

Pico reacts, guides, celebrates, worries, jokes, and grows alongside the player.

### 4. Progress feels like travel

CEFR progression is represented through independence, social complexity, and access to new cities.

### 5. Every interaction has context

No question exists without a reason inside the world.

## Target Experience

- Platform: browser-first responsive prototype, later mobile and native expansion
- Runtime direction: React Three Fiber inside the existing React application
- Camera: third-person exploration camera
- Session length: 10–25 minutes
- Tone: hopeful, cozy, curious, playful
- Audience: teens and adults learning English from A1 to B2

## Player Verbs

Primary verbs:

- walk
- look
- listen
- speak or choose dialogue
- inspect
- collect
- give
- buy
- use
- follow
- help

Secondary verbs:

- customise
- photograph
- replay
- practise pronunciation
- review vocabulary

## Core Loop

1. Explore a location.
2. Notice a person, sign, object, or problem.
3. Listen or read in English.
4. Decide what to say or do.
5. Receive an immediate world reaction.
6. Complete a quest step.
7. Earn XP, trust, vocabulary, or access.
8. Unlock the next part of the city.

## CEFR Progression

### A1 — First Flight

The player can survive simple travel situations.

- introductions
- numbers and prices
- signs
- food and drink
- basic directions
- present simple
- short questions

### A2 — City Explorer

The player can manage routine urban life.

- shopping
- recommendations
- transport problems
- describing needs
- simple past and future
- longer NPC exchanges

### B1 — Local Resident

The player can handle independent life and unexpected situations.

- renting a room
- making friends
- appointments
- complaints
- storytelling
- opinions
- natural accents at moderated speed

### B2 — Global Citizen

The player can participate confidently in complex social and professional situations.

- interviews
- negotiation
- debate
- humour and idioms
- phone calls
- collaborative projects
- varied regional accents

## Learning Rules

- Gameplay remains in English.
- French and Arabic are optional support languages only.
- Help never replaces the English interaction.
- Correct answers should produce visible world consequences.
- Incorrect answers should create kind, useful feedback—not punishment.
- Grammar is introduced through situations, not chapter labels.
- Repetition must appear naturally across different contexts.

## Emotional Goals

Every session should create at least three of these feelings:

- discovery
- belonging
- courage
- humour
- relief
- pride
- curiosity
- affection for Pico

## Non-Goals

Smart Parrot is not:

- a flashcard app with a 3D background
- a sequence of full-screen quizzes
- a clone of another entertainment property
- a photorealistic simulator
- a crowded dashboard surrounding a small play area

## Quality Bar

The world must do most of the teaching. UI should support the scene, not replace it.

A successful screen should make a new viewer say:

> This looks like a real adventure game.

Only after playing should they realise how much English they practised.
